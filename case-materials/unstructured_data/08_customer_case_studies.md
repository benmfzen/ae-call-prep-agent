# Customer case studies

*For AE reference · use as proof points in discovery and demo*

Short, specific, named. The best reference call is one your prospect can recognise themselves in — match on industry, size, and pain shape.

## Lumen Retail Group (DACH, Retail, 1,200 employees)

**Background.** Lumen runs 180 stores across Germany and Austria. Previously on a Meridian on-prem instance set up in 2014, plus three different workflow-automation providers. Triggered by a board mandate to consolidate operations systems following a 2024 acquisition of a 400-person hospitality chain.

**Why they chose Nimbus.** Native DACH workflow automation, modern UI that part-time store staff could actually use, faster implementation timeline than Vantive's bid (10 weeks vs. 9 months quoted), and an exec sponsor who had used Nimbus at a previous company.

**Outcome.** Live in 9 weeks. Workflow runs cleanly across both entities. Ops team reports ~40% reduction in time spent on workflow prep. Net Promoter from employees went from 22 to 41 in the first year.

**Use as a reference for:** retail customers, DACH multi-entity, post-acquisition consolidation, Vantive displacement.

## Sable Hospitality (Iberia, Hospitality, 650 employees)

**Background.** Hotel group with 14 properties across Spain and Portugal. Heavy shift-work operational overhead — over 65% of staff are on variable shift schedules. Previously used a custom-built tool plus paper-based setup.

**Why they chose Nimbus.** Shift tracking integrated with workflow automation, Spanish and Portuguese compliance native, ability for property-level managers to handle their own staffing without funnelling through head office.

**Outcome.** Live in 8 weeks. Setup time dropped from 14 days to 3. The Head of Business Operations runs a 4-person team where before she ran 7 — the additional 3 were absorbed into customer-facing roles. Saved approximately €280k in annualised operations cost.

**Use as a reference for:** hospitality, shift-based workforce, Iberia, mid-market hospitality with multi-location complexity.

## Cascade Logistics (Nordics, Logistics, 1,800 employees)

**Background.** Pan-Nordic logistics player. Complex regulatory and reporting requirements across four countries, plus a fragmented set of country-specific workflow tools that made cross-border reporting slow and error-prone. Previously on a patchwork of local point solutions with no shared data model.

**Why they chose Nimbus.** Multi-country workflow automation under one roof (Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland), strong handling of local data-residency and regulatory requirements, ability to model country-specific approval rules without custom engineering.

**Outcome.** Live in 14 weeks (longer than typical due to multi-stakeholder sign-off across four local entities). Eliminated three legacy vendors. Compliance reporting now takes a single afternoon instead of three days per cycle. Passed a Norwegian regulatory data-handling audit six months post-deployment.

**Use as a reference for:** logistics, Nordics, multi-country workflow automation, regulated multi-entity environments, compliance-heavy buyers.

## Vela Software (UK, Tech, 320 employees)

**Background.** B2B SaaS company. Scaled aggressively post-Series-B (140 → 320 seats in 18 months). Previously on Kestrel; outgrew the workflow-automation integration as seat count grew across UK + Ireland + Spain.

**Why they chose Nimbus.** Native workflow automation across all three countries, analytics integrated for high-volume onboarding of new seats, approval-cycle module ready for their first formal approval cycle.

**Outcome.** Live in 7 weeks. Setup flow now scales with seat growth without adding ops headcount. Approval-cycle participation went from 65% to 92% in the first cycle on Nimbus (vs. last cycle on Kestrel).

**Use as a reference for:** scaling tech companies, Kestrel displacement, multi-country expansion.

## Lost deal: Petrichor Industries (DACH, Manufacturing, 2,800 employees)

We don't typically share this one with prospects, but the AE team can use the pattern for their own deal strategy.

**Background.** German industrial manufacturer. We were one of three vendors evaluated alongside Vantive and a niche manufacturing-specific platform.

**Why we lost.** They had Vantive already deployed in finance, and the CFO led the selection. We were never going to win that one given the bundle pressure and incumbent advantage. The deal also had no clear catalyst — they were evaluating because it was "due time," not because anything was broken.

**Lessons.**
- Vantive-already-in-finance + CFO leading = qualify carefully before investing.
- No buying trigger means slow process and incumbent advantage. Trigger > stated interest.
- Champion was Head of Business Operations, but she didn't have political weight against the CFO. Multi-thread to Finance leadership earlier.

## How we use these in deals

- In discovery, surface 1–2 references that match the prospect's shape *before* they ask. ("There's a logistics customer of ours — Cascade — who had a very similar multi-country compliance challenge. Want me to set up a call?")
- In demos, reference the case study at the moment that's relevant to the slide. Specifics beat generalities.
- In proposals, include 2–3 brief reference quotes inline, with permission. The reference team has these on file.

Reference quotas: each customer agrees to a fixed number of reference calls per year. Don't burn references on low-probability deals.
