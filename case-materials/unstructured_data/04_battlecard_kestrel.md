# Battlecard: Nimbus vs Kestrel

*Last updated: 2026-04*

Kestrel is our closest direct competitor in European mid-market. We see them most in tech-flavoured SMB/lower-MM deals (100–700 employees), particularly in UK and Israel-anchored buyers.

---

## Headline positioning

> Kestrel optimises for the *adoption* layer of work-ops. Nimbus optimises for the *operating* layer. We win when customers need operations that actually run — workflow automation, compliance, approval cycles, analytics — not just usage analytics and an account structure that looks good.

---

## When we win

- Customer has real workflow complexity (multi-country, multi-entity, shift-based + fixed-schedule staff)
- Customer cares about compliance and audit trail (especially GDPR + data residency + country-specific regulation)
- Customer needs analytics integrated, not bolted on
- Customer values implementation discipline and account team continuity
- CFO or COO is involved in the decision (we're more credible as an operations system)
- Customer has tried Kestrel and outgrown the operations side

## When we lose

- Smaller SMB (100–300) where UI and culture features matter more than ops depth
- Customer is "Kestrel-first" culturally — UK tech founders, often
- Decision led by an operations leader who already has Kestrel loyalty
- Demo-driven decision (Kestrel demos *very* well)
- Cost-sensitive deal at the lower end of our ICP

---

## Their pitch (what they'll say)

- "Beautiful UX, employees love it" → true, design is genuinely good
- "Quick to set up" → true, faster than Vantive, comparable to Nimbus
- "Strong on usage analytics and adoption features" → true, where they invest most
- "Modern company, modern operations" → marketing line
- "Usage analytics built in" → reasonable, varies by module

## Our counter (what to say back)

- We're the system operations actually run on. Workflow automation, compliance, audit, analytics — first-class, not afterthoughts.
- European-first: native compliance across major European markets. Kestrel partners or integrates for workflow automation in most markets — that's a different operational model.
- Implementation discipline: we have a methodology, you'll have an implementation manager, you'll go live on the date we agreed.
- Analytics + core platform in one system, not two systems integrated. Important when the analytics pipeline feeds setup directly.
- We're the platform you grow into. Customers don't typically outgrow Nimbus in the 250–2,500 employee range. They do outgrow Kestrel.

---

## Talk tracks for specific objections

**"Our employees love how Kestrel looks"**
Fair, the UI is good. So is ours — worth a side-by-side in the demo. But: employees use the platform a few times a year. Your ops team uses it daily. Optimise for who actually has to make it work.

**"Kestrel is faster to implement"**
We're typically similar timeline (6–10 weeks). The difference is what's *running* at the end of implementation — with us, workflow automation, compliance, analytics are all live. With Kestrel, workflow automation is usually a separate vendor integration on top, which is where timelines slip.

**"We don't need all the operations stuff"**
Today, maybe not. In 18 months, when you're 1,400 people across three countries with a workflow error costing you €40k in goodwill, you will. Worth talking to a few customers about what changed for them at growth inflection points.

**"Kestrel is cheaper"**
Sometimes. Worth pricing the full picture: core platform + workflow automation + analytics + integrations + implementation. We usually come out within 10% — and ahead on TCO once workflow automation is in scope. Want to put real numbers on this?

---

## Discovery questions

- "Are you also evaluating Kestrel? What's drawing you to them?"
- "What's the workflow situation today? In-house, outsourced, multi-country?"
- "What's the analytics motion? Volume, geography, how many tools?"
- "Who in the company will use this daily? Weekly? Monthly?"
- "What's your view on having the core platform and workflow automation on separate systems?"

---

## Note on positioning

Don't trash Kestrel. We respect them, they're a good product. We win on operational depth and European fit, they win on adoption-layer features and SMB design. Saying "Kestrel is bad" makes us look insecure. Saying "we're the system that runs your business, not the system that tracks how people use it" lands.
